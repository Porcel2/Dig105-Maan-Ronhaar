# Dispute Resolution

Effective date: 8 November 2026

If something goes wrong with a job, the payment stays protected while both sides are heard.

## 1. Reporting a problem
The customer can report a problem from the booking at any time before the payment is released, which is up to 72 hours after the visit. Reasons include: the provider did not show up, the job is not finished, poor quality, being charged more than the quote, or damage to the home. The customer describes the problem and adds photos. Reporting puts the payment on hold straight away.

## 2. Timeline
| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Provider is notified and can reply with their side and photos | Within 48 hours |
| Both sides can agree a solution in the app: the provider comes back to fix it, or offers a partial refund | Any time |
| If there is no agreement, a Kodu specialist reviews the evidence | Within 5 working days |
| Decision with a written explanation sent to both sides | Same day as the review |

## 3. Evidence we look at
1. The job description, the quote and any approved change orders.
2. The chat, in both the original language and the translation.
3. Photos from the customer, and the provider's before and after photos, which are required when marking a job as done.
4. Arrival and completion times recorded in the app.

## 4. Possible outcomes
1. Release the payment to the provider in full.
2. Ask the provider to return and fix the work at no extra cost, with the payment released after the fix is confirmed.
3. Partial refund to the customer, with the rest released to the provider.
4. Full refund to the customer.

Damage to the home that costs more than the job is handled between the customer and the provider, or through the provider's liability insurance. Kodu shares the contact details needed to make a claim.

## 5. Appeals
Either side can ask for a second review by a different specialist within 14 days of the decision. After that, consumers can contact the Estonian Consumer Disputes Committee, and providers can use the mediation route set out in the Provider Agreement. Using Kodu's process never removes anyone's right to go to court.

## 6. Fair use
Opening disputes in bad faith, for example to avoid paying for work that was done, can lead to account limits. Providers are not penalised for disputes that are decided in their favour.
